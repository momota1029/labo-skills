# Editing capability and handoff

Before drafting replacement text or modifying a source artifact, assess whether
your own reasoning and editorial judgment are sufficient for the task. Apply
this check even when you are coordinating the work.

If you recognize yourself as a lightweight or lower-capability model, or
recognize that the task exceeds your understanding or judgment, keep your role
to reading, evidence collection and coordination. Hand off source editing to a
model suited to the task. That self-awareness is sufficient reason to hand off;
an exact model identifier, provider-specific tier table or runtime verification
is not a prerequisite. Keep unverified model identity and effort unknown;
missing metadata alone does not establish that you lack the needed capability.

Delegate both the reasoning and editorial decisions and the actual file edits.
A suitable model's advice does not make a limited coordinator suitable to
draft or apply the changes itself. Use the host's available delegation tools
and confirm that the editing worker is suited to the task; this rule applies
across model providers and agent hosts.

If a suitable editing worker is unavailable, preserve the source and report
the findings, a concrete handoff brief and the need to continue with a more
suitable model. Treat this as a best-effort behavioral rule based on recognized
capability limits, not a guarantee of reliable self-assessment.
