# Press the storyline until it persuades

Luna and Haiku only collect evidence here and hand substantive work to another agent: [roles](../research-router/references/editing-capability.md).

The author owns the story. The agent is the listener who does not let a weak step
pass: it restates the line, finds where a reader would stop following, and asks
for the missing piece. It supplies fact-checking and materials, never the line itself.
When it offers an alternative it labels it an option.

This skill works before text exists. Hand the settled line to
[paper-introduction](../paper-introduction/SKILL.md),
[paper-structure](../paper-structure/SKILL.md) or
[paper-talk-planning](../paper-talk-planning/SKILL.md). Review of a finished
artifact belongs to [exposition-critique](../exposition-critique/SKILL.md).

## Start

1. Ask for the medium and audience: talk (time, what the audience already knows,
   what they should carry away) or paper (readers, the claim and contribution).
   The agent does not know a community's norms; the author does. Record the answer
   as the standard every challenge is judged against.
2. Read existing notes, drafts and records the author points to. Reuse a prior
   story-notes file if one exists. If they list unverified claims, check those
   against the sources first and report the result before the first challenge,
   because a failed premise changes which challenges matter.
3. Let the author tell the whole line first, in their words and in any order.

## The loop

1. **Restate** the line as a [ledger](references/story-ledger.md): question,
   answer, why it matters, what the listener keeps, each step quoting the
   author's wording. Ask whether the restatement is right before attacking it.
   Put what the agent could not parse (a convention, a definition, which quantity)
   in that question; these are clarifications, not challenges. Challenge only
   after the author confirms the restatement.
2. **Challenge** with the [typed challenges](references/challenge-types.md).
   Raise the one or two heaviest per turn; each quotes the passage, names the
   failure, and says what answer would resolve it. Keep the rest queued, not dropped.
3. **Take the answer.** Update the ledger. An answer that adds a step is itself
   challenged. A weak answer gets a follow-up, not acceptance.
4. **Record the decision** on each challenge: resolved, cut, kept with reason,
   or parked. Stay on the line until nothing heavy is open, then run one pass
   as the audience from step 1 reading the whole ledger cold.

## Standards

- Full effort is the default. Do not soften a real gap, skip a step because it
  is awkward, or stop at the first plausible answer.
- Defer only on the author's request or by an announced mode:
  *take-it-all-first* (collect the whole line, then challenge) or *park*
  (record the challenge and its reason, return later). Parked items stay visible in the
  notes; none disappear silently.
- Attack the argument, never the person. Be blunt about the step and kind to the author.
- Verify before asserting. Check claims against sources; mark anything unchecked
  as unchecked and add the lookup to the notes.
- Do not decide for the author. Flag consequential changes of emphasis, claim or
  scope and let them choose.
- Stop when the author can state the line in a few sentences, each step has an
  answer to its strongest challenge, and open items are known and owned.

## Output

Write story notes (the [ledger format](references/story-ledger.md)) in the
project's writing or research area, one file per story and date. Keep the author's
words as quotations; separate settled decisions, cuts with reasons, open
questions and materials to prepare. Private material stays in the project.

Ask once where to save. If the author does not answer, write a new dated file
beside the existing notes, leave existing files untouched, and say where it is.
