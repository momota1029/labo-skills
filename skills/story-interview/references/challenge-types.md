# Typed challenges

Each challenge quotes the author's passage, names the failure, and states the
answer that would resolve it. Judge against the medium and audience recorded at
the start. Order by how much of the line collapses if the challenge stands.

| Type | Trigger | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Gap | The listener cannot get from step A to B with what they have been given | "From 'X' you move to 'Y'. What licenses it? Give the missing step." |
| Concretize | A claim stays abstract: "a better invariant", "a new viewpoint" | "What would I see or compute that I could not before? One example." |
| One-sentence test | The point of a section cannot be said briefly | "State this section's point in one sentence." |
| Cut | A definition, background or digression is not used by the line | "Nothing after this uses 'D'. Drop it, or name the step that needs it." The author decides. |
| Audience question | A member of the stated audience would interrupt | "Someone who knows [stated background] asks '…'. What do you answer?" |
| Strength | A claim outruns its evidence, or a hedge hides the real claim | "You say 'always'; the evidence covers Z. Narrow it or supply the rest." |
| Order | A term is used before it is motivated, or motivation arrives after it is needed | "'T' appears here but is justified two steps later." |
| Significance | The answer is clear but the reason to care is not | "If this is true, what changes for someone working on …?" |
| Fact | A statement depends on a source not yet checked | Check it. Report: confirmed with locator, contradicted, or unverified. |

Pressing follow-ups: if an answer restates the claim in other words, say so and
ask again with a different angle (an example, a counter-case, a consequence).
If an answer is good, say what it settled and move on.

Do not ask for definitions of terms the audience already shares; calibrate with
[exposition-granularity](../../exposition-granularity/SKILL.md) when the audience's
vocabulary is in doubt.
