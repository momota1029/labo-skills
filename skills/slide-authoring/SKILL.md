# Make the mathematical path visible

Before substantive work, follow [eligibility and handoff](../research-router/references/editing-capability.md).

For each user request, follow the [required consultation](../research-router/references/consultation.md).

When author identity, voice, or preferences matter, consult the Labo workspace
[author profile](../../../profile/author-identity.md) when available. Keep
personal facts there rather than in skill text; update only explicit durable
facts or corrections from the user, or facts they ask to save, with source
context. Apply this skill's normal verification and publication rules.

When the user wants to edit a concrete deck, inspect a preview, and use their
feedback to tune both the deck and its authoring skill, follow
[`skill-tuning`](../skill-tuning/SKILL.md) for the iteration and checkpoint.
Use this skill for the slide-specific audience, mathematics and pacing
decisions; do not turn one deck's preference into a general rule without
evidence from the feedback.

Use for talks, seminar slides, paper introductions, reading decks and their
reviews. Read [slide standards](references/slide-standards.md). Infer the
audience, prerequisites, language, purpose, source version and output format
from context. Distinguish a live talk from a deck intended for reading alone.
Use supplied talk/Q&A time; do not invent a duration or slide quota.
When the target's repository supplies a more specific slide workflow, follow
it for production and review roles, frozen artifacts, builds and records. Use
this skill's authoring and audience criteria within that workflow instead of
starting a parallel review loop.

For talks about the author's own research that assert novelty, significance
relative to prior work, or other substantive comparisons, run the required
[research-positioning checkpoint](../reading-reference/references/research-positioning.md)
before selecting that narrative. Reuse the program's memo, refresh affected
comparisons, and carry exact attribution and notation/convention choices into
the visible claims. This does not turn a paper-reading deck into a new research
program or require a whole-field survey for a layout-only edit. In review-only
work, inspect the displayed sequence first, then check the positioning evidence
and return findings without editing the deck or memo.

## Authoring

1. Identify the question and payoff, then select the claims and evidence that
   carry them. For the central turns, make the audience's question or likely
   expectation visible, show what example, comparison or result changes it,
   and state what follows. This is the deck's source of interest: a recoverable
   intellectual turn with a payoff. Use it where the story turns; setup and
   evidence frames can do their local job without a separate hook. Work
   backward to indispensable concepts and forward to their first consequential
   use. Specialize faithfully when generality would bury the idea. For a paper
   introduction, give an early map of its question, contribution, mechanism
   and limits. In an explanatory deck, add one concise preview line to the
   preceding frame when it helps orient the audience to the next explanation.
   Skip overview and summary frames, and avoid repeating a transition already
   made explicit; keep the preview in the author's voice.
2. Keep the selected theorem's hypotheses, objects and status visible. Explain
   the decisive step or usable black box; ordinary proof details may go to
   speech, backup, a paper or `reading/` notes. **Paper-referee completeness
   rules do not govern slides.** A reading deck may need more explanation
   than a short talk; preserve its requested coverage.
3. Put actual figures, examples and meaningful comparisons beside the claims
   they support. Retain source locators and distinguish evidence from schematic
   illustrations. For source collection or substantial reconstruction, use
   `reading-reference`; preserve its source-fidelity checks without importing
   a fixed reading workflow.
4. Preserve the existing editable format and use its available build tools.
   Deliver the requested source/PDF or other format, inspect the current render,
   and check the audience's route through the deck. Keep generated files under
   `output/` in labo. Slide preparation does not authorize manuscript edits.
5. Preserve the author's established voice and information, including
   self-introductions, informal asides, humor and stylistic quirks. Do not
   remove them solely for generic polish or brevity. When clarity, accuracy or
   layout requires an edit, keep it local and preserve the author's intent; do
   not invent personal details or jokes.

## Review

Read the displayed sequence before sources or speaker notes. Record the claim
and reasoning recoverable by the declared audience, including the first missing
bridge. For the central story, trace the visible question or expectation to the
result that answers or changes it and its stated consequence; report a missing
payoff only when the sequence gives concrete evidence for that gap. Then compare
with the exact source. Keep initial difficulty distinct from later source
rescue. Review-only requests return findings without edits.

Use numbered `S` criteria with severity, frame/overlay, visible evidence,
source locator when relevant, and minimum repair. Preserve correct concise
passages; a review can be clean. Report inspected scope, rendering limits and
whether time fit was estimated or rehearsed. For an isolated audience test,
give a fresh reader only the deck and neutral audience brief; derive the
assignment from this task, without a fixed persona, model or review roster.
For an independent slide reviewer, use an artifact-first, evidence-led
challenge: test the strongest plausible misreading and trace the central
question to its visible result and consequence, then check the named sources.
Ask for exact frame evidence and the smallest repair, while preserving a
concise correct frame as a control. Follow the project's source-access rules;
a source-aware reviewer and an isolated cold reader have different packets.
Do not set a criticism quota.
