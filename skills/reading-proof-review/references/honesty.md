# Rules that keep a reading page honest

These apply to every page this skill makes: a proof review, a proof book, a companion, a prose
explanation, on the site or standalone. Kind-specific additions are in the references of that kind
(the indicator's caps, the reproduction levels of a companion).

1. **Locators and status.** Every claim carries a locator in its source. Mark each statement as
   confirmed, suspected or unchecked; never write "correct". The strongest allowed statement is
   "no gap found within the stated scope". Keep what the source states apart from what the page
   reconstructs to make it readable, and say which is which.
2. **Who checked what.** State it near the verdict or the top. Reviewers that are language models
   are not an expert's check or a formal proof, and the page says so. A Lean proof certifies only
   the formal statement as written; whether that statement is the source's claim is a separate
   check by a person, and the page says whether it was done.
3. **Sources actually read.** Novelty and comparison are judged only against sources that were
   read; an unread source is stated as unread, never filled in from memory. For someone else's
   paper, the novelty claim is the paper's own claim, and a gap found in it is reported, not
   repaired.
4. **Provisional classes.** The class of a step (key, semi-key, standard) stays provisional until the
   prior-work stage; the page may change it.
5. **Impressions are not measurements.** An effect nobody counted ("it helped", "it got easier") is
   written as the author's impression. Do not turn it into a number or a comparison the page cannot
   support.
6. **The source is untouched.** Corrections found by the review are listed for the author to
   accept; edit only after agreement.
7. **Private stays private.** The page, ledger, findings and spec of a real project stay in the
   project's private workspace. A public destination needs its own request and the public-edition
   rules of [website-publishing](../../website-publishing/SKILL.md); only generic rules and
   fictional fixtures belong in this skill.
8. **Outcomes are distinct.** Drafted, built, reviewed by an independent reader, and published are
   different states; report the one that is true.
