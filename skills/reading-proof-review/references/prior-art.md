# Prior-work comparison

Compare with a **fixed, short list** of primary sources agreed with the user. Do not widen the list
on your own; a wider search is a separate decision, and the page states that the list was limited.

For each source record, in a row of the comparison table:

- how the equation or object is set up (operator placement, setting);
- the assumptions and the conclusion;
- where the source uses the same or a nearby argument as the key steps (page and equation number),
  or, when nothing is found, which sections and statements were actually read;
- what was checked: read in full, read in part, searched by keyword, abstract only, or **unread**.

## Unavailable sources

When a source cannot be obtained (paywall, missing file), say **unread**, do not describe its
contents from memory, and do not stretch a neighbouring paper to stand in for it. If the unread
source is the closest work, make that the page's largest unchecked gap, near the top: novelty
cannot be called strong until a person reads it, and the indicator rules cap it.

## Attack, then concede

Give a fresh reader (see [proof-adversarial-review](../../proof-adversarial-review/SKILL.md), the
prior-work viewpoint) the key steps and the source list and ask it to break the novelty claim.
Report overlaps first. When nothing is found, report the sections read, not "no overlap exists".
List what the source already concedes (for example "no novelty is claimed for this sign lemma").

Names of additional works that reviewers raise from memory go in a separate "candidates, bibliographic
details unchecked" list; they are suspicions until read.
