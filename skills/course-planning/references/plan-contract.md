# A usable book plan

Use these fields to make a plan actionable; adapt its prose and file shape to
the existing project. A template full of unanswered headings is not a plan.

## Overall route

Keep the destination, candidate books and their main prerequisite connections
compact. Distinguish mathematical dependencies from the author's preferred
production order. Ordinarily only the selected book needs a detailed plan now. If the user requests
a complete production design, give concrete plans for that bounded set of books,
without scaffolding every possible future specialization. Preserve
source-adoption obligations when assigning work to later books.

## Selected book

- **Reader and entrance:** expected mathematical maturity, permitted starting
  facts and conventions, explicitly accepted axioms and established results, and
  the exact adopted content to construct/prove. Do not infer inputs from a label
  such as foundational or advanced.
- **Question and endpoint:** what problem connects the chapters, and what the
  reader can understand or do at the end.
- **Scope:** adopted results, constructions, examples and necessary exercise
  solutions; explicit deferred material and its destination.
- **Reading route:** each part's question, inputs, output and next use; explain
  significant transitions instead of listing topics only.
- **Existing material:** canonical file/label locations and reuse, split, merge
  or relocation decisions; missing bridges and proof obligations.
- **Prerequisites:** owning file/label and version, the statement actually used,
  whether the proof is established, and any unresolved compatibility question.
- **Size:** observed current output versus estimated final output, including
  preliminaries, appendices and indexes; the uncertain growth factors.
- **Completion:** evidence that adopted content and prerequisites are closed,
  required self-check and selected review findings are handled, and matching
  output is available. Planning, writing, review and publication are distinct states.
- **Next action:** a bounded task, owner when known, and permitted writes.

For existing courses, include a current-passage to proposed-home mapping. Do
not equate an old planning ID with a physical chapter or completion status.
Do not treat the presence of a source section in a ledger as proof completion.

## Preliminaries and source ownership

Apply [volume entrances and borrowed results](preliminaries.md) when designing or
writing a volume entrance; it owns the operational details below.

Prefer the existing claim/source records to a parallel catalog. For each result
actually crossing a book boundary, record enough information to locate the exact
statement, owner and version, and classify it as established, incomplete, or
unproved. Keep assumptions and normalization visible. Introduce finer graph
tracking only where it resolves a concrete closure or change-impact question.

Define the book's self-containedness relative to that explicit input boundary.
Preliminaries provide usable definitions and exact accepted statements; the body
or bundled material proves the newly adopted content. An input's proof may remain
at its identified source if that is the agreed boundary. Do not promote missing
promised proofs into inputs without a recorded scope change. Unresolved inputs
leave dependent work conditional.
For an assembled work, declare its common inputs separately: every constituent
import outside those inputs must have an internal proof owner. The same rule
applies to introductory and specialized texts. Reusing a theorem in preliminaries
does not transfer ownership of its proof.

## Integration plan

Identify the versions to assemble and the intended route. Record outstanding
boundary conflicts: duplicated definitions, potentially equivalent theorems,
different assumptions or normalizations, forward dependencies and missing
transitions. Resolve equivalence from actual statements and uses before merging
labels or proofs. Give each result one editable owner and retain a mapping when
its location changes. A stronger replacement is a mathematical change.

Plan a reading pass across boundaries, claim/use checks affected by edits, and
integrated reference, index and output checks. Reuse valid checks of unchanged
scopes. The current book can finish while later books remain in the overall
route, but the collected course cannot finish with unresolved required proofs.

## State and placement

The course entrance points to the overall plan, current volume, canonical text
and output, and records whether execution is stopped. Volume progress owns its
details; course progress selects the current work and links to those details.
Review cases retain their immutable inputs and findings in their existing homes.
Only create volume or integration files needed for the authorized work. Do not
create a second reviewed edition or a new global completion ledger.
